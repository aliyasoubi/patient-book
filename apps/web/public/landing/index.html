How a case moves

From the chair to the lab and back.

Each step hands the next one what it needs, so nobody has to remember to copy a date from one book into another.

  2. 01

    Open the patient file

    Find anyone in a keystroke — by name, phone or file number, in Persian or Latin digits.

  3. 02

    Record the surgery

    Implant or extraction, tooth position and system, linked to the right register entry.

  4. 03

    The follow-up schedules itself

    Months after surgery become a real Jalali date, due for the whole month and overdue only after it.

  5. 04

    Send the work to the lab

    Turnaround days give an expected return; a missed day turns the case amber on the board.

  6. 05

    Deliver and close

    Back at the clinic, booked, delivered — and the history stays on the patient’s file.

Security & reliability

Medical records deserve better than “probably fine”.

  • Four staff roles

    Admin, dentist, receptionist and read-only viewer. The front desk sees clinical notes but cannot change them.

  • Encrypted off-site backups

    AES-256 before anything leaves the server, plus restore drills — a backup you’ve never restored isn’t one.

  • HTTPS, strictly

    HSTS and a strict content policy. No CDNs, no analytics, no third-party scripts reading your screens.

  • Your server, your data

    Runs on the practice’s own server. The database is never exposed to the internet.

  • No silent overwrites

    If a colleague saved first, your edit is stopped instead of quietly replacing theirs.

  • Guarded sign-in

    Rate-limited login, short-lived sessions, and a forced password change for every new account.
